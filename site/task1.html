Task 1 / Technical view

ROS 2 group communication.

The implemented system lets multiple ROS 2 clients join one hub, exchange messages, see lifecycle events, and share one bounded history.

← Back to the interactive overview
Problem

Keep every terminal informed.

Clients need identity, join and leave events, shared messages, active-user counts, and a small history without direct client-to-client coupling.

Built

One owner for shared state.

The hub assigns IDs and colours, validates sender ownership, stores the latest records, and publishes a single broadcast record for every client.

Architecture decisions

Small contracts. Clear ownership.

Why a hub? It provides one validation point and one consistent broadcast record.

Why services? Registration and departure need a response. They are not streams.

Why topics? Chat, system events, and telemetry are continuous data.

Why readiness checks? A process can exist before its ROS 2 service is discoverable.
